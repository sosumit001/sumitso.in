AI is supposed to make you faster. What nobody warns you about is that it also makes it easier to stop thinking, and by the time you notice, you’ve already handed over the parts of your work that made it yours.

What This Article Covers

**Losing Control**: how convenience turns into blind trust

**Learning New Skill**: why skipping the process breaks the outcome

**Irreversible Decisions**: when a mistake can’t be undone

**Outsourcing Judgment**: the muscle you stop using without noticing


## Don't Outsource Your Thinking

### Losing Control

It's very easy to reach a point where it's too late for you to think about putting in manual effort, and you let AI take over every single decision.

This is the point where you need validation for every single question that is bugging your brain. What AI is actually doing is just answering based on its training data, reasoning from data based on tools connected to a true source of information, and predicting an outcome that a common human brain might require a lot of effort to reach.

You see, the possibilities are numerous, and humans aren't built for that. We humans are lazy, we can't work 24/7. I call this "Losing Control," where you just let yourself go with the vibes and accept everything AI produces for you.

This turns into an illness for people who question AI outcomes. They feel pressure to deliver fast, but at the same time, they can't accept the fact that AI is actually nailing their work. And it triggers them when someone points out a weakness in what they've done.

So it's a very relevant question to ask, when should you not use AI? And the answer is definitely not straightforward.

### Learning New Skill

If I go back a few years and someone asked me to build a rocket, I would do a lot of research on the materials, the design, the combination of several components, and finding the right fuel so it could launch successfully.

Learning is all about gathering useful information and molding it into a working model, and that's where new discoveries happen, after running lots of experiments and building a solid foundation that can be followed and improved over a very long period, until someone creates a very different alternative to it.

Learning is a very interesting process, and one thing you definitely can't rely on is consuming misinformation. Today we have this magical information filtering machine that does its job really well in finding resources and saving your effort. The only issue is when you ask this machine to build a rocket.

#### The Process

Your milestone is definitely the product. The only issue with that product is that you're not following the process, you're losing control instead.

The process should be:

1. Find relevant resources
2. Extract the right information
3. Build the rocket that works
4. Run experiments
5. Launch it, when it's ready

The machine should be your worker, helping you along the way to reach your milestone. But the main problem arises when you start feeling more like an investor who isn't aware of the process, someone who is just there to put in cash to reach the milestone.

The ROI for this magical machine is taking control, controlling your personal information, all your judgment, and promising you a rocket you can launch. There are numerous reasons for letting that control go.

### Irreversible Decisions

AI will always present its outcome in the best possible form when one is required, but it is definitely not responsible when that outcome is tested in the real world.

There are two kinds of outcomes:

1. Reversible
2. Irreversible

Outcomes can be corrected only if they are reversible, for example, a bad email, a wrong headline, a poorly worded tweet. You fix it, and you move on. But some decisions won't give you that luxury.

Irreversible outcomes are always the result of approving everything AI decides for you. Maybe you're not a domain expert and questioning feels out of place. Maybe there's pressure from other directions to produce quickly, and you don't care enough about the consequences in the real world.

There's a muscle you use every time you question something before accepting it, every time you pause and think, "does this actually make sense?" That muscle needs practice. And AI, without you noticing, slowly takes that practice away from you.

### Outsourcing Judgment

When every question gets an instant, confident answer, you stop forming your own opinion first. You skip the step where you would have thought it through yourself. The part of your brain responsible for catching a wrong answer gets weaker because you've stopped using it.

This is dangerous because you don't notice it happening. You still feel productive. You're still shipping work, answering questions, and moving fast. But you've quietly outsourced the one thing that made your work reliable, which is your own judgment.

If you can't explain why an AI's answer is right, you didn't actually verify it, you just accepted it. And if you can't remember the last time you disagreed with AI and were right, that's not proof AI is always correct. It's proof you've stopped checking.

## When Not to Use AI

So far, I've told you many reasons why you shouldn't rely on AI too much, but the main topic still remains uncovered: when not to use AI.

Throughout writing this, I asked people across different domains one simple question: when do you personally draw the line with AI? Here's what came back.

### **When the outcome is irreversible**
We can talk about this all day, but the bottom line is, you should never let AI outcomes affect other people. Sharing misinformation with the outside world is the last thing you'd want to do.

### **When you are the expert and AI is generic**
I use AI all the time, but I never let it assume anything. I've seen examples where humans just trust AI-generated output before validating it. Even for validation, there are AI bots that can't be trusted.

### **For medical decisions, especially mental health**
Decisions directly connected to your life shouldn't be left to a machine. AI is built for assistance, and the feedback it provides won't consider the several other factors a human would recognize.

### **When you're still learning the fundamentals**
Starting directly with AI will never connect you with the history of the domain you're working on, because it will always try to give you the best possible answer, not the process that leads there.

### **When trust is the actual product**
If the thing you're delivering depends on someone trusting you personally, a lawyer's advice, a friend's opinion, a mentor's feedback, letting AI write it for you quietly breaks the thing that made it valuable in the first place.

### **When you can't explain your own decision**
If someone asks you why you made a call and your honest answer is "the AI said so," that's the moment to stop. You should always be able to defend a decision in your own words.

### **When speed is the only reason you're using it**
There's a difference between using AI to move faster and using it to avoid thinking. If you can't tell which one you're doing, you're probably doing the second.
